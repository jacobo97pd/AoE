## Command and destination planning

`World.Submit` validates a nonempty selection of at most 1,024 owned, existing, distinct units and sorts it by stable entity ID. `MovementSystem.Submit` checks the raw target's map bounds, static walkability, radius clearance and connectivity for every selected unit. A click inside a static obstacle or without sufficient radius clearance rejects; a walkable click occupied by an idle unit can resolve to nearby free slots.

`FormationPlanner` reserves destinations independently of route search. `MoveCommand(playerId, unitIds, destination, formation = MovementFormation.Loose)` exposes the optional formation; `UnitState.Formation` records the accepted mode. Invalid enum values reject before mutation. Preferred spacing is `max(cellSize, 4 * largestRadius + cellSize / 2)`: for the one-metre grid and 300 mm radius fixture, this is 1,700 mm, leaving a nominal half-cell corridor for a same-radius mover's center between regular settled rows.

| Mode | Preferred layout |
| --- | --- |
| Loose | Preserve current offsets around the selected center. A spatial-grid nearest-pair check expands the cohort only when its closest pair is below the desired spacing; sufficiently loose groups translate without repeated fixed-scale growth. |
| Line | One lateral row perpendicular to the dominant cardinal travel axis. Ties choose the X axis; a zero-distance order uses positive X as its reference. |
| Box | Approximately square grid with `ceil(sqrt(count))` columns and enough rows, oriented by the same dominant cardinal travel axis. The last row may be partial. |

If any preferred slot is outside the map, statically invalid, occupied by an idle body or reserved by another moving group, a bounded free-ring search replaces the preferred layout. A single-unit fallback prefers cardinal candidates before diagonal candidates at each ring. Therefore the requested mode is an intent, not a promise to preserve an obstructed shape. Preferred Loose keeps each unit's offset assignment; Line, Box and fallback assignment preserve lateral and front-to-back order with stable-ID ties. All paths must still succeed before accepting the order.

Every destination must clear static geometry, idle nonselected bodies and reserved destinations of other moving groups. The entire selection's paths must succeed before movement/task state changes. Rejected commands can populate diagnostic/cache state but do not partially move the selection or cancel its tasks. Accepted orders retain the requested raw target, formation mode, group identity, group size and per-unit destination. Repeating the same intact group's target and mode preserves its slots and progress; changing mode replans. Stop cancels the group and leaves an immovable idle body; other routes and automatic combat acquisition clear stale group identity before assigning new movement.

## Static navigation and shared fields

`Navigation` uses a four-neighbour blocked-cell grid and connected-component prechecks. Cell centers provide safe cardinal passages for supported unit radii, which are at most half a cell. Exact radius and segment clearance are checked again during movement. Ordinary route search is breadth-first; queue, visited stamps and predecessor arrays are reused, and path lists are rented from `RouteLists` and returned after use. The route search, the group path over a flow field and the local detour expand neighbours along the direction of travel: first towards the destination, then away from it. They do not use a fixed compass order. Among equally short routes, a trip and its 180-degree rotation therefore choose rotated cells, so the mirrored starts of the match maps move alike. An axis-aligned trip breaks the tie towards the map centre. Before 13 September 2026 the fixed order favoured movement towards +x and +z by one or two ticks per trip.

Selections of at least four use a reverse breadth-first distance field centered on the group's requested target cell. Individual routes descend that field, preferring the candidate closest to their start-to-slot line when distances tie. Near the formation footprint, a final breadth-first route reaches the individual's assigned destination. Groups below four use ordinary routes. If an anchor becomes unusable but an individual's destination remains connected, route search can fall back to the individual destination.

Each navigation instance retains at most **four fields**, keyed by anchor cell with recent-use eviction. Field builds and cache hits remain inside the path-query timing bucket. Every connectivity rebuild clears the complete field cache, including after adding a foundation, destroying a building or exhausting a resource. This is synchronous whole-cache invalidation; there is no separate revision-number cache protocol or incremental affected-region update. Construction preview owns a separate navigation instance and cache, while sharing opt-in metrics.

New foundations and destroyed buildings synchronously replan active routes. An unsuccessful replan retains the movement intent, clears the unusable route and waits until the retry tick. Depletion frees its resource cell and clears shared fields; existing valid routes may continue, with later requests using the opened cell.

## Per-tick collision and avoidance

`UnitSpatialGrid` is a reusable uniform-grid broad phase. Each tick indexes initial body bounds, then adds each committed movement sweep. Queries deduplicate unit indices with stamps. Old positions remain indexed while later units move, so checks include both unmoved bodies and already committed sweeps. Initial overlapping unit circles reject world construction. New production spawns also check existing swept bodies before appearing.

Units are stored in stable ID order. The movement loop rotates its first processed index by `TickIndex % unitCount`; this changes local priority deterministically instead of giving the same first actor every tick. A unit receives its integer speed budget with a fractional remainder. Route smoothing skips intermediate waypoints only when the full swept disc clears static geometry. Temporary recovery paths also require body clearance when smoothing, preventing a shortcut from discarding the detour around an idle unit.

The visibility optimization reuses the chosen straight segment until it is reached, sidestepped or replanned. Choosing a new segment requires at most **12 visibility checks**: test the final waypoint, then use exponential lookahead and bounded binary refinement if it is obscured. This can conservatively retain a shorter safe segment; it is not an exhaustive search for the farthest visible waypoint. Full immediate-step collision checks still run every tick. Static changes replan active routes and reset their segments, so reused smoothing state does not authorize movement through a new obstacle.

`MovementGeometry` checks relative-motion swept discs for unit pairs and swept discs against blocked-cell rectangles. Tangency is legal; penetration is rejected. It uses integer arithmetic with a decimal fallback when the intermediate products would overflow `long`. Position bounds include the entire unit radius. A rounding guard keeps movement within its tick budget plus the declared integer-rounding allowance. A committed move is checked against nearby bodies before changing position; idle bodies are not pushed aside.

If forward motion is blocked, local avoidance tests a fixed set of heading rotations using integer sine/cosine tables. It scores forward progress with a consistent side preference. Backward directions become available after 12 blocked forward attempts or while yielding. A higher-ID mover that remains blocked by a lower-ID moving unit can retain a reverse yield heading for 30 ticks. Rotating processing order and this temporary yield rule are deterministic heuristics, not a proof that every arbitrary crowd arrangement will converge.

## Recovery and work limits

After at least **eight blocked forward attempts**, a unit may request local recovery if its cooldown has elapsed. There are at most **four local recovery queries per world tick**, and a given unit waits **20 ticks** before another request. The current query uses a fixed **25 by 25 sample lattice**, at most **625 visited samples per query**, at half-cell spacing approximately six world cells around its current position. On the one-metre grid, this is 500 mm sampling across a 12 m span. Bounds/static clearance reject samples outside the map. Every explored step checks static swept clearance and nearby bodies. It chooses a reachable point closer to the final goal, or the final goal when available; the original final destination remains authoritative while the temporary route is followed. The finer lattice addresses legal center corridors skipped by the earlier one-metre recovery sampling.

Missing or exhausted routes retry no more often than once per 20 ticks. The four-query budget applies specifically to local recovery. Full route submissions, synchronous foundation/destruction replanning, connectivity rebuilds and combat chase queries are not covered by one global scheduler or latency budget. Combat pursuit retains its separate five-tick cadence per attacker. Instrument these costs separately; there is no asynchronous request backlog to report in the current implementation.

## Economy and combat integration

Workers may stop a movement route and begin work from a valid adjacent footprint cell when their center is at most half a cell from the footprint. Approach searches exclude a candidate occupied by another idle body. This allows workers to gather, deliver or construct from free positions without requiring all of them to occupy one exact approach point. Cargo credit still requires arrival beside a valid owned completed drop-off, and movement changes preserve carried resources. Destroying a targeted building halts its workers and either clears construction work or lets carriers find another drop-off.

A recruit appears on the free edge cell nearest its building's rally point, or nearest the map centre when the building has none. Until 13 September 2026 it took the first free cell of a fixed sweep, which always started at the building's lower-left corner. That sent recruits from mirrored bases out on different sides. Equal interaction approaches likewise prefer the cell nearer the worker, then the map centre. Production sends each spawned unit through the movement destination resolver for its rally order, so later arrivals receive nearby free goals instead of waiting forever on the first unit's occupied point. An unavailable rally route leaves the newly created unit at its legal spawn; it does not undo the completed training transaction. Automatic combat acquisition clears previous group identity, allowing a subsequent explicit move back to the old requested point to execute after a chase.

Phase 6 movement consumes each unit's effective speed, including owner-specific cavalry and Reposition modifiers. Reposition suppresses attacking during its active window but allows Move/Stop; a deployed Threadkeeper rejects both commands. Deploying outpost transports reject Move until Stop cancels deployment. These restrictions reject mixed invalid selections atomically. Packing disables drop-off immediately; completion replaces the building with a population-zero movable transport under the same entity ID and clears its footprint. Redeployment requires a legal adjacent footprint and waits at zero remaining ticks if occupancy prevents completion. Successful replacement restores the footprint and replans routes while preserving health and identity. These faction interactions have rule/integration coverage, but the neutral scale matrix does not measure their active workload.

## Acceptance and remaining limits

The reproducible matrix covers 50, 100, 200, 300 and 500 movers across OpenField, WideCorridor, NarrowChoke, CrossingGroups, DynamicObstacle and Unreachable. Normal 100-unit open/wide/crossing cases must finish by the declared 120-second deadline with no final stalls or overlaps and no persistent pair overlap; every sampled case must avoid static/map violations. Unreachable orders must reject atomically and remain still. Other sizes and constrained cases expose arrival and congestion outcomes even when they are outside the required normal-case gate.

The observer's stall metric detects immobility, so circling units may show zero stalls. Arrival fraction and the deadline remain essential. Endpoint observations are complemented by independent swept-circle and static-rectangle rule tests. CPU timings exclude observer work; the historical Phase 4 packaged frame timings include the 10 Hz observer, explicit URP submission, forced canvas updates and synchronous one-pixel GPU readback. That serialized offscreen throughput belongs to its recorded Phase 4 measurement build and does not profile Phase 5 research or Phase 6 faction behavior/UI. Shared-field builds/hits, local recovery counts and visited cells explain route costs without conflating a cached request with a fresh full-grid search. Allocation availability must pass the known-allocation probe described in the methodology; Phase 6's known-allocation probe still reports the counter unavailable.

The implementation remains synchronous and allocates route/field data. The final matrix demonstrates unresolved persistent congestion in the larger one-cell choke cases, despite safe positions and reduced CPU cost. Broader completion guarantees, larger maps, heterogeneous formations and mobile hardware need their own evidence. Cross-runtime deterministic replay verification remains future work; fixed ticks and integer positions alone do not establish it.
