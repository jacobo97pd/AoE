# Emberfield — game vision

Status: design foundation. See [current phase](../tasks/CURRENT_PHASE.md) for delivered features.

## Player promise

Build a small refuge into an influential realm during one readable, tense match. Explore the borderlands, decide where workers are safe, spend scarce resources, raise an army, and adapt when scouting reveals a rival's plan. Each competitive match resets economic and military power; what persists is the player's understanding.

Emberfield is an original working IP set in a fictional landscape recovering from recurring ash seasons. Communities cultivate warm river terraces and compete for the roads, stores and high ground needed to sustain them. This premise supports civic infrastructure and mobile frontier factions without reproducing historical civilizations or another game's lore. All names remain working creative names pending any later release clearance.

## Audience and platform

Primary: iPad and Android tablets. Secondary: iPhone and Android phones. Desktop input exists to develop and test the same game. The product is stylized 3D with an elevated, initially fixed-rotation RTS camera.

Desired match lengths after tuning: casual 8–15 minutes; standard competitive 12–20 minutes; extended custom play 20–30 minutes. These are targets, not observed outcomes. No enforced timer should replace strategic closure without playtest evidence.

## Pillars and decisions

| Pillar | Player decision | Design test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growth with exposure | More workers, another production building, or an army now? | Economic investment has a discoverable period of vulnerability. |
| Information changes plans | Scout, defend, raid, or advance an Era? | At least two plausible responses follow a useful scout report. |
| Readable combined arms | Protect ranged units, hold a line, or flank? | Unit role and target are readable at normal zoom. |
| Territory has value | Keep safe income or expand to richer exposed resources? | Leaving the base offers a benefit and a risk. |
| Native touch command | Select and command a group with a few deliberate gestures. | Strategic intent survives thumb input and small displays. |
| Fair repeat play | Learn a timing or composition for the next attempt. | Account age and purchases never alter standard PvP stats. |

## Product layers

Build the RTS core first. Fair results and skill matching come after an enjoyable offline match. Visible rank, mastery, cosmetics, useful post-game analysis and seasons are later layers. Keep their data separate from match balance so cosmetic ownership cannot affect damage, armor, speed, costs or income.

No paid gameplay power, energy system, construction wait monetization or account-level combat upgrades. Potential later cosmetic purchases or content packs need separate product review and competitive parity; there is no payment integration in early phases.

## First playable experience

The current greybox supports selection, movement, gathering, drop-off, construction, training and combat. See the current phase record for the complete select-to-fight loop and counter verification evidence. Group movement and congestion are the next gate. Final art and networking cannot compensate for an unclear command or uninteresting economic choice.

The eventual first session teaches one action at a time: camera, selection, movement, food gathering, construction, training, a small fight, Era progression, victory, and then an AI skirmish. Feedback should explain the result of the player's own action before introducing another system.

## Success and risks

Judge success through observed completion of the core loop, command accuracy, match finish rate, readable losses, and device frame stability. Do not invent numerical retention goals before collecting a useful baseline.

Main risks: attention overload on phones, congestion during group orders, economy snowballing before meaningful interaction, Era investment becoming mandatory at one fixed timing, and faction differences multiplying balance costs. Address them with incremental tests, limited content, clear feedback and recorded playtests.
